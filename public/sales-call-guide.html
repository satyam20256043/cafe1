Zordical — Phone Call Script & Conversation Guide

For calling café/restaurant owners. Read once fully, then keep the Cheat Card (bottom) open during calls. Lines in quotes are meant to be said almost verbatim — but say them like yourself, not like reading. Mix Hinglish naturally if the owner does.

For the strategic why behind this script — especially the feature→benefit reframing habit — see Pitch Playbook. Same rule applies everywhere below: never say "AI chatbot" or "CRM," always say what it does for their business.

Call goal, in order of preference:

  1. Sign them up live on the call (walk them through zordical.com/onboard together)
  2. Book an in-person visit / demo at their café
  3. Permission to send the pitch link on WhatsApp and a fixed follow-up day

Never end a call without one of these three.

0. Before you dial (60 seconds)

  • Look up the café on Google Maps: rating, review count, photos, busy times. One specific detail = ten generic pitches.
  • Have open on your screen: zordical.com/pitch, zordical.com/onboard, this script.
  • Ready to send on WhatsApp: the pitch link, zordical.com/pitch.
  • Smile before you dial — it genuinely changes your voice.

1. The opener (first 20 seconds decide everything)

Cold call:

"Hi, am I speaking with the owner of [Café Name]? ... Great — my name is Satyam, I run a small tech company called Zordical. We help cafés get more repeat customers using WhatsApp. I'm not selling a POS machine, I promise. Do you have 3 minutes, or should I call at a better time?"

Why it works: names their café (not a random list call), says the benefit in one line (repeat customers — every owner wants this), the POS joke disarms, and asking permission respects their time. If they say "busy" → "No problem — is 4 PM okay, or tomorrow morning?" (offer two options, never "when are you free?").

If a lead came from the website/referral:

"Hi [Name], you'd checked out Zordical online / [X] gave me your number — calling to personally answer any questions. What made you curious about it?"

Then go straight to Discovery — they'll tell you their pain themselves.

2. Discovery — 3 questions, then LISTEN (minutes 1–3)

Ask, then stay silent. Whatever pain they name becomes your whole pitch.

  1. "Quick question — when a customer WhatsApps you at 11 at night asking about a table, what happens to that message?"
  2. "Are your weekdays as busy as your weekends, or is there a slow day that bugs you?"
  3. "When a regular customer stops coming — do you even get to know, or do they just quietly disappear?"

Note their answer word-for-word. You will repeat their own words back in the pitch — this is the single most persuasive thing you can do on a phone call.

3. The pitch (minutes 3–6) — lead with THEIR pain

Bridge line (always):

"So what Zordical is — think of it as an AI receptionist that lives inside your WhatsApp number. Your customers message the same number they already have. The difference is: now every message gets answered in seconds, 24 hours a day."

Then pick the ONE lane matching their discovery answer:

Lane A — they miss messages / bookings:

"That 11 PM message you mentioned? The AI answers it instantly — menu, prices, timings — and if they want a table, it books it, asks their name, guests, time, and it's on your dashboard before you wake up. You lose zero bookings to slow replies."

Lane B — slow weekdays:

"You said Tuesdays are dead. Every Monday morning Zordical looks at your actual sales and sends you one suggestion — like 'Tuesday 3–6 PM, 15% off, send to your regulars.' You tap approve once, it goes to your customers on WhatsApp. That's your marketing department, for the price of a few coffees."

Lane C — customers disappear:

"Zordical quietly notices every regular who hasn't come in 2 weeks, writes them a personal WhatsApp message with a small offer and a coupon code, and — this is the part owners love — you SEE who actually came back and spent money. Not likes, not views. Rupees."

Be accurate about Lanes B and C: sending an offer to many customers at once uses the official WhatsApp Business connection (we set it up with them). Quick QR connect handles replies, not bulk offers. If they ask, say exactly that — never promise more.

The safety line (use in every call — it kills the biggest fear before they say it):

"And important — the AI never gambles with your reputation. A complaint, a refund, a big group booking, anything it's not sure about? It calms the customer politely and immediately alerts YOU on WhatsApp with a suggestion. Big decisions stay yours. Even discounts — you set the maximum it's allowed to give, per item if you want."

If they ask "what else does it do?" — the 30-second full tour:

"Full picture: AI receptionist on WhatsApp · QR codes on tables so guests order without waiting for staff · orders go straight to a kitchen screen · automatic loyalty points so visitors become regulars · win-back messages for lapsed customers · a weekly growth suggestion from your own sales data · reviews — happy customers get nudged to Google, unhappy ones come privately to you first · and a dashboard where you see every order, every rupee, every conversation, live, from your phone."

Numbers (say slowly, they're the hook):

"For a café doing about 60 customers a day, even our conservative math says ₹40–50,000 extra a month from repeat visits and saved bookings. The plan most cafés take is ₹3,000 a month. It pays for itself if it saves ONE table a week."

4. Objections — phone versions

"I'm doing fine without it."

"That's honestly great to hear. Can I ask just one thing — [repeat their discovery answer, e.g. 'you did say night messages go unanswered']. If fixing only that was free for 30 days, worth a look?"

"AI will say something wrong to my customers."

"Right instinct — here's the design: anything sensitive, it doesn't answer. It says 'let me check with the owner', and pings you. And you can watch every single conversation it has, live, in your dashboard. Nothing is hidden from you."

"Too costly."

"What does an average table give you — ₹400? ₹500? So ₹3,000 is six tables a month. The win-back messages alone usually bring more than six people back. And the first 30 days are free with no card, so the risk is literally zero."

"I'm not technical."

"Perfect, it's built for you then. If you can use WhatsApp, you can use Zordical. I personally help you set up, step by step — including the WhatsApp connection, which is the only technical step, and I do that WITH you."

"Send me details, I'll see." (the polite brush-off)

"Of course, sending right now on this number. But so I send the right thing — what's the one thing you'd want it to prove before you'd trust it?"

Their answer = the real objection. Handle THAT, then close again.

"Let me ask my partner/wife/brother."

"Makes sense. What do you think THEY'll ask? ... Let me give you the answer to that right now so you're ready." Then: "Should I call back tomorrow evening after you two talk — 7 PM?"

5. Closing (minutes 6–8) — momentum, not pressure

Best close — sign up together, on the call:

"Tell you what — the signup takes literally 2 minutes and the first 30 days are free, no card. Open zordical.com/onboard on your phone, I'll stay on the line and we'll do it together right now. Then you can play with it tonight."

Walking them through it live is worth 10 follow-up calls. While on it: name → owner name → phone → done → "See that username and password? Screenshot it right now." → show them the dashboard opens.

Second close — book the visit:

"Actually, better idea — I'm in [area] on [day]. Give me 15 minutes at your café, I'll set up your real menu in front of you and you can see a live order on your own table. [Day] morning or evening?"

Soft close — locked follow-up:

"No problem. I'm sending you the 2-minute visual demo on WhatsApp right now. Have a look tonight, and I'll call Thursday at 5. If it's not for you, tell me straight, no hard feelings."

6. After the call — WhatsApp within 5 minutes (while you're warm in their memory)

If signed up:

"Great talking, [Name]! 🎉 Your café is live. Your login: zordical.com/login/[cafe-id] I'll call tomorrow to help connect your WhatsApp — that's the last step. Welcome aboard! ☕"

If thinking:

"Good talking, [Name]! As promised: ▸ 2-min demo: zordical.com/pitch The trial is 30 days free, no card. Calling you [day] at [time] as agreed. — Satyam, Zordical"

If no:

"Thanks for your time today, [Name]. No follow-ups from me — but if slow Tuesdays ever start bugging you, this number works. All the best with the café! ☕"

(A graceful no today is a warm lead in 3 months. Owners talk to each other.)

Didn't pick up — voicemail/missed-call text:

"Hi [Name], Satyam from Zordical here — we help cafés like [Café] turn WhatsApp messages into bookings automatically. Sending a 2-min demo link here. Will try you once tomorrow — or just reply with a good time. ☕"

7. Phone manner — the 8 rules

  1. Stand up or sit straight — your voice carries energy or the lack of it.
  2. Slow down. Nervous = fast. Trustworthy = unhurried.
  3. Pause after questions. Count 3 in your head. Silence does the selling.
  4. Their name, 2–3 times across the call. Not more (creepy), not zero (cold).
  5. Never talk over them. The moment they speak, you stop, even mid-word.
  6. Match their language. They go Hinglish, you go Hinglish.
  7. One lane per call. Their pain, your fix. The other features are for later.
  8. Log every call — name, pain, objection, next step, date promised. A promised callback made ON TIME converts better than any script line in this file.

8. Never do these (they backfire, simple as that)

  • Claim a feature that doesn't exist or a client that doesn't exist.
  • Invent urgency ("only 2 spots left"). The free trial and the math are enough.
  • Argue with a hard no. Thank, exit warm, log it.
  • Quote a discount you haven't decided you can honor.
  • Bad-mouth Zomato/Swiggy/competitors — say instead: "we don't replace them, we make sure YOUR OWN customers come back to YOU, without commission."
